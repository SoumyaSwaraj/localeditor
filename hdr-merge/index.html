HDR merge

Merge bracketed photos into one HDR picture.

Take the same scene at different exposures and merge them into one picture that keeps detail in the highlights and the shadows. The merge aligns the frames, can remove moving things, and then you tone-map the result in the same studio.

What it does

  • Merges two to nine photos, all RAW or all JPEG or PNG
  • Aligns frames that moved a little between shots, including a small rotation
  • Ghost removal at three strengths for people and clouds that moved
  • Two ways to merge: true HDR radiance, or exposure fusion
  • Tone mapping afterwards, with the merged picture kept as an ordinary image in your design

How it works

  1. Open the studio in Expert mode and choose File, then Merge to HDR.
  2. Pick the bracketed photos, two to nine, then wait while the merge runs on your device. You can cancel.
  3. Adjust the tone mapping in the panel and download.

Your files stay on this device

The merge runs in a background process in your browser on this device. Photos are never uploaded, and the merged picture is stored in your design on this device.

Questions

Short, honest answers.

More on the privacy and install page.

Does it work for handheld shots?

It handles small shifts and small rotations, up to about three degrees. A larger move, or a step sideways, can fail; a tripod, or exposure fusion with ghost removal turned high, works better then.

How big can the merged picture be?

The merge is limited to about 8 megapixels on a computer, 5 on a mid-range device and 3 on an older one, because it needs a lot of memory. Larger photos are reduced for the merge.

Can I use RAW brackets?

Yes. RAW frames are merged before white balance, so the result can be developed afterwards. Mixing RAW and JPEG frames in one merge is not allowed.

Is this the same as the HDR look on one photo?

No. A single photo has less to merge. The tone mapping control can still give a single RAW photo an HDR-style look.
