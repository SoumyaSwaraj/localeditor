Colour grading

Grade photos with curves, wheels and LUTs.

Expert mode has the colour tools photographers and colourists use: tone curves, a colour mixer with eight hue bands, three-way colour wheels, white balance in kelvin and your own .cube LUT files. Every change is a setting you can adjust, switch off or remove later.

What it does

  • Tone curve with separate red, green and blue curves, and hue-based curves for selective changes
  • Colour mixer: hue, saturation and brightness for eight colour bands, plus black and white mixing and a channel mixer
  • Colour wheels for shadows, midtones and highlights, lift gamma gain, colour balance and a gradient map
  • Import a .cube LUT, or start from a built-in look such as teal and orange, warm film or silver mono
  • Histogram, waveform and vectorscope beside the picture, with a before and after view

How it works

  1. Open the studio in Expert mode and add a photo.
  2. In the panel on the right, open Curves, Colour mixer or Colour grading and move the controls.
  3. Compare with the original in the before and after view, then download.

Your files stay on this device

Photos are edited by your browser on this device, using your graphics hardware. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and nothing is added to what you export.

Questions

Short, honest answers.

More on the privacy and install page.

Does it change my original photo?

No. Every adjustment is a setting stored with the photo layer. Switch it off, reset it or remove it and the original comes back.

Can I use LUT files made elsewhere?

Yes, .cube files, both 1D and 3D. A file that is over 16 MB, damaged or uses unknown entries is refused with a message instead of being half read.

Can I grade only part of a photo?

Yes. Each adjustment can have a mask: a brush, a gradient, or a range of brightness or colour. An adjustment layer grades everything beneath it.

Does it work on my phone?

It opens on a phone with a compact layout, but Expert mode is designed for a larger screen. The effects need WebGL 2, which most current phones and computers have; the Device check page shows what your browser supports.
