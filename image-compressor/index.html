    What is an Image Compressor?

    An image compressor makes picture files smaller so they load faster on websites, fit email attachment limits and use less storage. This tool decodes each image in your browser, draws it onto an HTML canvas, optionally scales it down to a maximum width, and re-encodes it as JPEG or WebP at the quality you set (10–100).

    Lossy compression at 70–80% quality usually cuts photo size by 50–80% with no visible difference. WebP files are typically 25–35% smaller than JPEG at similar quality. Re-encoding also strips EXIF metadata such as GPS location.

    Your images never leave your device: there is no upload, no account and no file limit beyond your device’s memory, and the page keeps working offline once it has loaded.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is this image compressor free and private?

    Yes. It is 100% free with no sign-up or watermark. Compression runs locally in your browser using the canvas API, so your images are never uploaded to a server.

    What quality setting should I use?

    For photos, 70–80% is a good balance: files are usually 50–80% smaller and the difference is hard to see. Use 85–95% for images with fine detail or text, and 50–65% when the smallest size matters most.

    Should I choose JPEG or WebP?

    WebP produces files roughly 25–35% smaller than JPEG at similar quality, supports transparency and is displayed by all modern browsers. Choose JPEG when you need maximum compatibility with older software, email clients or print services.

    Why did my PNG lose transparency or get bigger?

    JPEG has no alpha channel, so transparent areas are filled with white; choose WebP to keep transparency. Images that are already heavily compressed may not shrink further, and the tool flags these so you can lower the quality or the maximum width.

    Can I compress many images at once?

    Yes. Add as many images as you like; they are processed one after another on your device. Change any setting and every image is recompressed automatically, then use Download all to save them.

    Does it remove EXIF metadata?

    Yes. Re-encoding through the canvas strips EXIF data such as camera model and GPS location, which makes files smaller and safer to share.
