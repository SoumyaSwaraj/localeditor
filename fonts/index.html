Why these shapes?

What research says about letter shapes.

  • Easy to read feels easier and truer. When text is easy to read, people judge the task as simpler and the statement as more likely true. The trust fonts are open, even and roomy for that reason.
  • Curves feel friendly, sharp angles feel tense. People prefer curved shapes to sharp ones, and match round lettering with sweet tastes and angular lettering with sour or bitter ones. The positive fonts are round; the tension fonts are cut and pointed.
  • Heavy reads as loud and strong. Weight is the quickest way to be noticed. The attention fonts are heavy, or very wide, or very narrow.
  • Handwritten feels human. Lettering that looks drawn by hand makes people feel a person is behind the product. It suits cards and treats more than instructions.
  • Hard to read is a choice, not a default. Shaky or unusual letters slow people down and lower trust. Use the tension fonts for a title or a warning, not for prices or terms.
  • Fit matters most. A font that matches the message is chosen more often and remembered better. That is why every font here says what it is good for.

The effects are real but small, and they depend on colour, words, pictures and culture. Most studies used English-language readers. No font makes anyone buy anything.

How they were made

A small program draws every letter from a set of design choices: how heavy, how wide, how round, how open, where the thick and thin parts fall, what the ends and serifs look like, how steady the line is. Each font is one set of those choices, checked letter by letter on screen. No outlines were copied from any other typeface.

They cover English and most Western and Central European languages, with prices in €, £, $, ¥ and ₹. Use them in anything you make with LocalEditor, including work you sell. The font files themselves stay inside LocalEditor.
