Relight and depth effects

Add light to a photo after you have taken it.

The app estimates how far away each part of a photo is, then uses that to add a light that falls on the subject properly, a rim light that outlines it, rays of light through a scene, fog that thickens with distance, and catchlights in eyes.

What it does

  • Lights you place on the picture: sun, bulb or spot, with colour, softness, shine and shadows
  • Rim light that outlines a subject from behind
  • Light rays, depth fog and background dimming
  • Catchlights in the eyes
  • Depth blur that blurs by distance

How it works

  1. Open the studio with the button and add a photo. The Light tool is ready once a photo is open.
  2. Choose Depth map. The app makes one on your device. Then add a light and drag it into place.
  3. Set its colour and softness, add a second light if you like, then download.

Your files stay on this device

Photos are edited by your browser on this device, using your graphics hardware. The depth map comes from a small model that downloads from this site once, runs on your device and is listed in the storage manager. Nothing you edit is sent anywhere.

Questions

Short, honest answers.

More on the privacy and install page.

How good is the depth map?

It is a small model’s estimate of relative distance, not a measurement. It separates a subject from the ground and the background well and gives faces and large objects a believable roundness. It is weaker on hair, thin things and flat walls, so keep light and relief settings moderate.

How big is the download and how long does it take?

The depth model is about 16 MB and downloads once from this site. After that a map takes a few seconds on a computer and longer on a phone. It needs a fair amount of memory while it runs.

Will it fix a badly lit photo?

It can add and shift light and soften light that is already there. It cannot remove hard shadows or bring back what was lost in dark or blown-out areas.

Does it work on my phone?

It opens on a phone with a compact layout, but Expert mode is designed for a larger screen. The effects need WebGL 2, which most current phones and computers have; the Device check page shows what your browser supports.
